## Sources & Further Reading {.unnumbered .unlisted}

The research referenced throughout this guide. If something caught your attention, these are the people and works worth exploring further.

**Goal-Setting & Planning**

- Edwin A. Locke & Gary P. Latham, *A Theory of Goal Setting and Task Performance* (1990); *Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey* (2002), American Psychologist. The foundational work on how goals direct attention and energise effort.
- Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky, *Intuitive Prediction: Biases and Corrective Procedures* (1979), TIMS Studies in Management Science. Why we systematically underestimate the time and cost required for our plans.
- Bent Flyvbjerg & Dan Gardner, *How Big Things Get Done* (2023). Analysis of 16,000+ large projects showing 91.5% go over budget, over schedule, or both.
- Roger Buehler, Dale Griffin & Michael Ross, *Exploring the "Planning Fallacy": Why People Underestimate Their Task Completion Times* (1994), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. We're better at estimating how long things will take others than ourselves.
- Richard Wiseman, *New Year's Resolution Project* (2007), Quirkology web experiment. Reported that 88% of around 3,000 resolution-setters failed despite many being confident at the outset.
- Strava, 2019 activity-data analysis, reported by Keith Marshall in *Strava reveals 2020 quitters day* (2020), Sports Insight. Its fitness-activity data informed a prediction that people would be most likely to give up their 2020 fitness resolutions on 19 January.

**Wellbeing & Motivation**

- Tim Kasser & Richard M. Ryan, *A Dark Side of the American Dream: Correlates of Financial Success as a Central Life Aspiration* (1993), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology; *Further Examining the American Dream: Differential Correlates of Intrinsic and Extrinsic Goals* (1996), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. Placing extrinsic goals (wealth, fame, image) above intrinsic goals is associated with worse psychological health.
- Helga Dittmar, Rod Bond, Megan Hurst & Tim Kasser, *The Relationship Between Materialism and Personal Well-Being: A Meta-Analysis* (2014), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. Links materialism to lower life satisfaction and negative self-image.
- Lawrence S. Krieger & Kennon M. Sheldon, *What Makes Lawyers Happy?: A Data-Driven Prescription to Redefine Professional Success* (2015), The George Washington Law Review. In a study of 6,200 lawyers, income, class rank, and partnership status had small or negligible correlations with wellbeing.
- Jennifer Crocker, Samuel R. Sommers & Riia K. Luhtanen, *Hopes Dashed and Dreams Fulfilled: Contingencies of Self-Worth and Graduate School Admissions* (2002), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin. Students who based their self-worth on academic competence experienced bigger rises and falls in self-esteem after acceptances and rejections.
- Kent C. Berridge & Terry E. Robinson, *Liking, Wanting, and the Incentive-Sensitization Theory of Addiction* (2016), American Psychologist. Research distinguishes dopamine-related *wanting* from *liking*, showing that motivation for a reward can grow without an increase in pleasure.
- Carly A. McComb, Eric J. Vanman & Stephanie J. Tobin, *A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Social Media Exposure to Upward Comparison Targets on Self-Evaluations and Emotions* (2023), Media Psychology. Experimental studies show that exposure to people presented as better off on social media tends to worsen self-evaluations and emotions.

**Learning & Achievement**

- Maurizio Zollo, *Superstitious Learning with Rare Strategic Decisions: Theory and Evidence from Corporate Acquisitions* (2009), Organization Science. Managers' perceptions of past acquisition success were linked to worse subsequent merger performance, a pattern weakened by systematically examining and recording what they had learned.
- Shmuel Ellis & Inbar Davidi, *After-Event Reviews: Drawing Lessons From Successful and Failed Experience* (2005), Journal of Applied Psychology. Soldiers who reviewed successes as well as failures improved their navigation performance more than those who reviewed failures alone.

**Identity & Narrative**

- Dan P. McAdams & Kate C. McLean, *Narrative Identity* (2013), Current Directions in Psychological Science. Your narrative identity is your internalised, evolving life story, and redemptive stories are associated with higher wellbeing and generativity.
- William L. Dunlop & Jessica L. Tracy, *Sobering Stories: Narratives of Self-Redemption Predict Behavioral Change and Improved Health among Recovering Alcoholics* (2013), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. Among newly sober participants, 83% whose last-drink stories contained self-redemption maintained sobriety at follow-up, compared with 44% of those without it.
- Mary H. Jacobsen, *Hand-Me-Down Dreams: How Families Influence Our Career Paths and How We Can Reclaim Them* (1999). On inherited family values that conflict with personal direction.

**Philosophical Traditions**

- Stoicism, *Premeditatio malorum*: mentally rehearsing loss before it arrives.
- Buddhism, the distinction between *taṇhā* (compulsive craving) and wholesome aspiration (*kusala-chanda*).
- Taoism, the Taoist farmer parable on the impermanence of fortune.
